Surfaces, tables and the shell

You said tables lack a background, that you want stronger kits for tables and cards, that Quicken is the touchstone, and that the app should not read like every other AI product. Below: the actual cause of the flat tables, what Quicken and its peers really do (pulled from Mobbin), and four decisions for you to make — shell, table kit, panel doctrine, and flavour.

Nothing is committed. Stage 1 of the money-flow plan is finished and passing (build ✓, 539 tests ✓, lint 0 errors) but sitting uncommitted in the working tree, exactly as you left it. These decisions change what Stage 1's pages look like, so it is the right moment to make them.

1 — The flat tables

It is one missing class, in 16 files

src/components/ui/table.tsx renders a bare <table> with no surface of its own. Every page then wraps it in <div className="rounded-md border"> — a border with no bg-card. On the gray canvas the rows are transparent, so the table is gray-on-gray inside a hairline. The header band is bg-muted/50, which on that canvas is very nearly the same gray again.

Tables are the only major surface in the app that never joined the card system:

2 — What the references actually do

Quicken does not render tables at all

I pulled Quicken's live web product plus five peers off Mobbin. The single most useful finding: Quicken's account lists are cards containing rows, not tables in a box. There is no grid, no cell borders, no gray header band — a white card, a title, hairline row rules, right-aligned money, and an inline delta chip. That is most of why it reads as a financial instrument rather than an admin panel.

4 — Decision B

The shell

Quicken's real web shell is a thin icon rail plus a wide data panel, not a labelled sidebar. That second column is the thing you are describing when you say you like sidebars for exposing detail — Quicken's is a live accounts list with balances, permanently on screen. All three options below carry the six-entry money-flow nav from Stage 1.

S1Labelled sidebar + contextual panelRecommended

Keep today's 240px labelled nav with its tone tiles and badges, and add a per-section context panel that Quicken-style carries live data: under Bank it is the account list with balances; under Income/Expenses, saved views with counts; under Tax, the period and its deadline. Collapsible, and sections that have nothing useful to put there simply do not render it.

5 — Decision C

Panel doctrine — three sidebars, three jobs

You said sidebars for detail and settings are core to how you want this to work. Right now the app has no rule for that, so each page invents its own. These are the three distinct roles; writing them into DESIGN.md is what stops the next twenty pages from each guessing.

P1Right detail drawer — inspect one record without leaving the list

Mercury and Midday both do this and it is the single biggest workflow win available: click a row, the record opens beside it, arrow keys walk the list, Esc closes. Docked (pushes content) rather than overlaid, so the row you are editing stays visible. URL-addressable via ?doc= so it is still deep-linkable. This would replace most navigations to /documents/[id]/review for quick edits, while the full review page stays for real extraction work.

6 — Decision D

Flavour — what stops it looking like every AI app

Being blunt about the cause: gray canvas + white rounded cards + one neutral accent + Geist + Lucide icons is the most-cloned aesthetic on the web right now. It is the default shadcn/Vercel look, and we adopted it faithfully. The palette itself is not the problem — Ink Neutral is a good decision for financial data. What is missing is any domain signature.

These five moves add that without spending colour, and without reopening the Ink Neutral decision. Each is independent — pick any combination.

F1 · Satang-split money

Baht at full weight, satang smaller and lighter. Mercury does this; no accounting SaaS in Thailand does. Reads instantly as a finance product and makes columns of figures far easier to scan.
